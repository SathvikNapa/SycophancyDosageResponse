REVISED EXPERIMENTAL PLAN FOR NAACL 2027
========================================

OBJECTIVE
---------
Strengthen the existing paper through improved statistical robustness, confidence-extraction validation, and behavioral-taxonomy reliability while retaining the original four research questions and corrected R=3 Chain-of-Thought pipeline.

Core benchmarks: MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, Humanity's Last Exam (HLE).
Target models: The six models in the corrected manuscript.
General approach: Prioritize reanalysis of existing data; add focused validation experiments where necessary. Account for repeated turns, questions, replicates, and multiple testing.

RQ1: HOW DOES BASELINE UNCERTAINTY RELATE TO SUSCEPTIBILITY?
-----------------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1.1 — Effect magnitude, direction, and heterogeneity
- For each model–benchmark cell, compute delta = P(flip | uncertain) - P(flip | certain).
- Report percentage-point difference, confidence interval, unadjusted and Holm-adjusted p-values.
- Summarize positive and negative associations, median and range of effects, and model/benchmark variation.
- Plot all 16 cell-level effects in a forest plot.
- Avoid claiming a reliable individual effect when no comparison survives Holm correction.

Experiment 1.2 — Pooled uncertainty effect
- Fit a pooled logistic/hierarchical model from question-level outcomes.
- Account for model, benchmark, and repeated observations of questions.
- Report average marginal risk difference, model- and benchmark-specific estimates, and heterogeneity.
- Treat new post-hoc pooled analyses as exploratory unless validated independently.

Experiment 1.3 — Single-turn robustness
- Retain corrected single-turn results.
- Contrast baseline uncertainty associations after the first intervention and across multiple turns.
- Check whether the direction is present independently of accumulated conversation.

RQ1 outputs: forest plot, pooled estimate table, heterogeneity analysis, single-turn check.

RQ2: HOW DO PRESSURE AND UNCERTAINTY JOINTLY INFLUENCE SUSCEPTIBILITY?
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Experiment 2.1 — Main effects and interaction
- Fit an appropriately clustered or mixed-effects logistic model with uncertainty, pressure, uncertainty × pressure, and model/benchmark effects.
- Use categorical pressure/turn terms unless a linear pressure effect is justified.
- Report coefficients, intervals, odds ratios, and marginal probability differences.
- Handle question-level dependence and replicated/longitudinal observations.

Experiment 2.2 — Interaction robustness
- Reproduce corrected benchmark interaction coefficients (MMLU-Pro 0.20; GPQA 0.03; HLE 0.45, as reported).
- Examine pooled and benchmark-specific interaction estimates when justified.
- Report uncertainty intervals and avoid conflating lack of statistical significance with evidence of no effect.

Experiment 2.3 — Persona, authority, pressure, and tone controls
- Run a 2 × 2 × 2 design: authority (none/expert), pressure (mild/strong), tone (cooperative/confrontational).
- Keep question, proposed answer, factual content, and evaluation procedure constant across matched conditions.
- Use multiple matched paraphrases and randomize prompts.
- Evaluate answer flips, post-intervention correctness, main effects, and preselected interactions (authority × pressure, authority × tone).
- Do not infer a self-presentation mechanism solely from observed cue effects.

Experiment 2.4 — Resistance versus stubbornness
- Compare misleading corrections to genuinely correct corrections on a smaller matched subset.
- Distinguish initially correct and initially incorrect response populations.
- Measure inappropriate capitulation and appropriate correction separately.
- Ask whether social framing improves discrimination or simply increases answer persistence.

RQ2 outputs: pressure-response plots, regression tables, persona effects, correct-versus-incorrect feedback results.

RQ3: ARE RESISTERS, CAPITULATORS, AND FRAGMENTERS STABLE BEHAVIORAL PATTERNS?
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Experiment 3.1 — Within-benchmark bootstrap stability
- Resample QUESTION IDs with replacement 2,000 times per benchmark.
- Preserve all R=3 replicates and turns for each sampled question.
- Recompute the exact behavioral metrics and reapply the original classification procedure each time.
- If classification is cluster-derived, refit clustering in each replicate and align cluster identities.
- Report frequencies of each model receiving each label, along with intervals for underlying metrics.
- Freeze or justify the classification rule; avoid thresholds chosen solely to produce desired groups.

Experiment 3.2 — Cross-benchmark stability
- Compare every model's labels on MMLU-Pro, GPQA, and HLE.
- Separate within-benchmark sampling stability from between-benchmark transportability.
- Test whether HLE changes classification; interpret changes as possible benchmark-conditioned behavior.

Experiment 3.3 — Fragmentation robustness
- Recompute fragmentation using corrected R=3 consensus definitions.
- Report eligible-question denominators and confidence intervals.
- Retain corrected entropy-versus-fragmentation analysis.
- Contrast Gemini's substantial HLE fragmentation with zero observed eligible HLE fragmentation for Claude models without universalizing the pattern.

Experiment 3.4 — Classification sensitivity
- Evaluate plausible threshold changes or cluster-specification choices.
- Consider pairwise co-clustering frequencies, adjusted Rand index, and leave-one-model-out sensitivity where appropriate.
- With six models, emphasize transparent model-level metrics over universal cluster claims.

RQ3 outputs: label-retention table, cross-benchmark transition table, fragmentation estimates and classification sensitivity.

RQ4: HOW DO CORRECTNESS AND REASONING-DERIVED CONFIDENCE EVOLVE?
-------------------------------------------------------------
Confidence is estimated by an EXTERNAL LLM from generated reasoning, not directly observed inside the target model. Use “reasoning-derived confidence” consistently.

Experiment 4.1 — Cross-evaluator validation
- Apply GPT-5.4 Mini and Claude Sonnet to the SAME reasoning traces with matched rubric and input visibility.
- Report Spearman correlation, mean absolute difference, absolute agreement (e.g. suitable ICC), and agreement on within-question direction of change.
- Compare scores and trajectories by target model and benchmark.

Experiment 4.2 — Paired confidence changes
- Compute delta C(i,t,judge) = C(i,t,judge) - C(i,0,judge).
- Separate answer transitions: correct→correct, correct→incorrect, incorrect→correct, incorrect→incorrect.
- Test whether both judges observe increases during correct→incorrect transitions.
- Report clustered uncertainty intervals and paired effect sizes.

Experiment 4.3 — Human expressed-certainty audit
- Sample approximately 150–300 traces stratified across models, benchmarks, score ranges, and correctness transitions.
- Have at least two blinded annotators rate certainty EXPRESSED IN THE REASONING.
- Compare human ratings to both external evaluators.
- Do not interpret this as validation of inaccessible internal model belief.

Experiment 4.4 — Evaluator-input sensitivity
- Compare reasoning-only, reasoning + answer, and full-context input conditions.
- Test prompt/rubric variations and sensitivity to assertive versus hedged wording.
- Identify possible contamination by adversarial context or rhetorical style.

Experiment 4.5 — Correctness/confidence divergence
- Plot paired correctness and reasoning-derived confidence trajectories by model and benchmark.
- Report joint transitions and, if assessing calibration, clarify that the score being calibrated is a JUDGE-DERIVED proxy, not directly measured target-model belief.
- Preserve the reported Claude Haiku/GPQA observation (correctness 0.646→0.025; extracted confidence 0.830→0.929), subject to cross-judge validation and correct inferential interpretation.

RQ4 outputs: judge-agreement table, human validation results, input sensitivity, joint confidence/correctness plots.

5. Freeze model versions, benchmark question IDs, prompts, sampling parameters, output extraction, confidence rubrics, exclusions, and analysis code.
